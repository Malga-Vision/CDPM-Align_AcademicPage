CDPM-Align:
Multi-Scale Guidance-Aligned Diffusion Pretraining for Robust Few-Shot Anatomical Landmark Detection

Roberto Di Via1, Irina Voiculescu2, Francesca Odone1, Vito Paolo Pastore1
1MaLGa-DIBRIS, University of Genoa, Italy    2Department of Computer Science, University of Oxford, UK
Paper (MICCAI) Poster (PDF) Code MICCAI 2026 — Accepted

Figure 1: Overview of CDPM-Align. Left: Two-phase multi-dataset pretraining with standard conditional diffusion training followed by alignment phase enforcing directional consistency of the guidance signal. Right: End-to-end few-shot fine-tuning via pixel-wise classification.

We introduce CDPM-align, a conditional diffusion pretraining framework with multi-scale guidance alignment. Pretrained on only 988 images, it achieves state-of-the-art few-shot landmark detection with sub-millimetre ERE and outperforms self-supervised methods in robustness.

Abstract

Anatomical landmark detection is a fundamental task in medical image analysis supporting a wide range of diagnostic and interventional workflows. Although recent methods have achieved sub-millimetric localisation, accuracy alone is not sufficient for clinical deployment, requiring reliability and robustness in prediction. Despite its clinical relevance, the impact of representation learning in this context is still underexplored.

In this work, we introduce CDPM-Align, a multi-scale guidance-aligned conditional diffusion pre-training for anatomical landmark detection. Our experimental setup focuses on a few images and a few annotation regimes. Specifically, we employ three popular heterogeneous small-scale benchmark datasets for representation learning via conditional generative pre-training. Furthermore, we consider low-annotation scenarios for the downstream task of landmark detection, with 10 and 25 annotated images, reflecting realistic trade-offs between clinical effort and resource constraints for annotations.

Our results confirm that generative pre-training enables the model to learn a robust representation. This improves both accuracy and uncertainty on the downstream tasks, advancing towards safe and efficient clinical deployment.

Key Contributions

  1. Conditional generative pretraining strategy: We introduce a conditional generative pretraining strategy for small-scale heterogeneous X-ray datasets, augmented with a multi-scale guidance alignment loss that improves localisation accuracy, uncertainty concentration, and robustness in low-shot regimes.
  2. Comprehensive validation: We validate the proposed method across three clinical dimensions (accuracy, predictive uncertainty, and robustness) on three public benchmarks under 10- and 25-shot protocols, showing consistent improvements over supervised, SSL, and state-of-the-art baselines.

Method

Conditional Diffusion Probabilistic Model (CDPM)

We build on denoising diffusion probabilistic models (DDPMs) with class-conditional generation via classifier-free guidance. The forward process produces noisy samples, and a noise-prediction network is trained with class labels (dataset indices). The backbone is a UNet with FiLM embeddings for timestep and class conditioning.

CDPM-Align: Multi-scale Alignment on the Guidance Signal

We introduce a multi-scale alignment objective that enforces directional consistency of the guidance signal across independently sampled diffusion timesteps and UNet hierarchy levels. For each image, we sample two timesteps and perform forward passes with conditional and unconditional labels.

The guidance difference Δh = h_cond - h_uncond encodes dataset-specific anatomy. We extract features at four hierarchy levels: encoder 1/4, encoder 1/8, bottleneck, and decoder 1/8. The alignment loss averages the per-level cosine dissimilarity between the two views.

The alignment phase is applied only in the final 10% of training iterations, reducing computational overhead from 4× to 1.3×.

Downstream Landmark Detection

Following pretraining, the full pretrained UNet is fine-tuned end-to-end with a pixel-wise classification head. During fine-tuning and inference, the unconditional class label is used, decoupling the downstream task from pretraining labels and enabling future application to unseen datasets.

Experimental Results

We evaluate on three public benchmarks: Shenzhen (279 chest radiographs, 6 landmarks), ISBI2015 (400 lateral cephalograms, 19 landmarks), and Digital Hand Atlas (DHA) (910 hand radiographs, 37 landmarks). The pooled unlabeled corpus comprises 988 images. Results are reported as mean±std over 5 independent runs.

Key Findings

  • State-of-the-art few-shot performance: CDPM-align achieves best MRE in 5/6 dataset-budget settings
  • Sub-millimetre ERE: At 25-shot, achieves ERE below 1mm on ISBI2015 (0.95mm, +25% improvement) and DHA (0.97mm, +34% improvement)
  • Robustness on heterogeneous data: SSL methods exhibit severe performance degradation on DHA at 10-shot (MRE 20-25mm vs 2.51mm for CDPM-align)
  • Data-efficient: Pretrained on only 988 images, it matches or surpasses models trained on 112k NIH radiographs
  • Key improvements: +22% on ISBI2015 10-shot, +42% on DHA 10-shot
